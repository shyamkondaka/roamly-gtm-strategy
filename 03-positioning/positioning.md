# Positioning Framework & Statement · Roamly Groups

## 1. Positioning framework

- **For (target audience):** The person who ends up planning and paying for a celebration trip (bachelor/bachelorette weekends and milestone birthdays) for 8 to 20 friends or family, in our launch cities.

They’re the one whose card is on the line. They’ve usually already agreed on the idea in the group chat, and they’re the one who will chase everyone else for money.
- **Who want / need:** To make a great plan actually happen without being the bank for everyone else.

In their words:

“I don’t want to put $1,500 on my card and chase people for weeks.”
“Everyone said they’re in. I need to know who’s actually paying.”
“I just want one place to see who’s in and who’s paid, and for it to be booked.”
- **We offer:** Roamly Groups is a way to book a local, hosted experience for a whole party through one link. Each person pays their own share, the organizer can see who’s in and who’s paid, and the booking is confirmed once the group is ready.

A stranger would understand it as: the organizer sends a link, friends pay for themselves, and the experience is booked.
- **Because of (value proposition):** Because each person pays for themselves at the moment of booking, the organizer gets a confirmed plan with none of their own money at risk and no weeks of chasing.

The outcome is relief and certainty: booked, paid, zero dollars fronted. Splitting and the shared itinerary support this but aren’t the claim.
- **Despite (barriers):** Naming these plainly, since each one becomes a message we have to handle.

We’re asking for new behavior when the free stack is already “good enough.” Venmo, Splitwise, Partiful, and the group chat cost nothing, everyone already has them, and the plan is usually agreed before Roamly enters. This is the biggest barrier.
Friends may not want to pay through a new platform. If the invite link feels like friction, the booking stalls. Our answer depends on a pay-without-an-account flow that isn’t built yet.
“Zero fronted” depends on a policy we haven’t finalized. If some friends don’t pay, someone absorbs the gap. Until we decide who, the headline promise can’t be made honestly.
A visible fee will be compared to free. We have to justify it as the price of removing risk, not of splitting.
We only cover Roamly inventory. If the group already wants a specific non-Roamly activity, we can’t help.
- **Better than (differentiation):** Better than the free coordination stack (group chat + Partiful + Splitwise/Venmo) for organizers of larger, costlier groups, because payment and booking happen in one action, backed by vetted local hosts.

Why it’s defensible	Why it’s not defensible alone
The free stack has no inventory, host relationships, or way to link payment to a confirmed reservation. Building that takes years.	Splitting, itineraries, and invitations are easy to copy. The edge comes from the combination, and only if the unpaid-share policy works.

For organizers of 8-20 person celebration trips, who need to make the plan happen without fronting everyone’s money, Roamly Groups is a way to book a hosted local experience for the whole party through one link where each person pays their own share. Because of that, the organizer gets a confirmed booking with none of their own money at risk. Despite being a new tool in a world where free apps are already on everyone’s phone, it’s better than the group-chat-plus-Venmo workflow because booking and payment are one action backed by vetted local hosts.

## 2. Positioning statement

### First draft
Roamly Groups lets you book a local, hosted experience for your whole party through one link, where everyone pays their own share. You get a confirmed booking without putting anyone else’s cost on your card or chasing friends for money.

Why it’s built this way
Offering first: the opening sentence says what it is and how it works (one link, everyone pays their own share), so a stranger understands it immediately.
Value second: the outcome is the organizer’s relief, a confirmed booking with nothing fronted and no chasing.
No jargon: it avoids “split payments,” “coordination,” and “platform,” because the free stack already claims those words.
A shorter alternative

Roamly Groups books a local experience for your whole party through one link, with everyone paying for themselves, so you’re never the one fronting the money.

### Pressure test
- **Test I ran:** B · Competitor discomfort
- **What it revealed:** What the test revealed

The statement described a mechanism, not a position. It told the reader how Roamly Groups works (one link, everyone pays their own share, reminders) and not why Roamly is the one to do it. A mechanism is exactly what a competitor can copy in a quarter. A product lead at Partiful could have lifted about 70% of it word for word, and nobody would have noticed.

Where it was too generic
“Through one link,” “everyone pays their own share,” “without chasing friends for money.” Each of these is something the free stack already does or could ship soon. They made the sentence sound like a better Splitwise.
“Without putting anyone else’s cost on your card.” This is the heart of the bet, but written alone it reads like a Venmo request. Nothing signals how Roamly makes it true.
Where it was too broad
“A local, hosted experience.” Every tour platform says this. It’s the only phrase the competitor can’t claim, and I buried it in soft, common language. The things that make Roamly’s hosts different (vetted, curated, the reason repeat booking runs at three times the industry average) were missing entirely.
“Your whole party.” This hides the narrowing I committed to. The bet is about 8 to 20 people, where fronting money really hurts, and the statement didn’t carry that.
Where it was unclear
“Confirmed booking.” It didn’t say when the booking is confirmed or what happens if someone doesn’t pay. The reader can’t tell whether the promise is real, and neither can I, because the unpaid-share policy isn’t decided.
“No chasing.” This is probably overclaimed. If confirmation waits on everyone, the organizer still nudges the late friend. Roamly can take over the reminders, but it can’t remove the social pressure.
What I learned about my own work
I wrote the first draft from the organizer’s pain and not from the competitor’s gap. The pain is real, but the free stack addresses it well enough that describing it doesn’t differentiate us.
The most defensible part of the position was the least visible. Supply, vetted hosts, and payment-linked booking were in the battlecard but not in the sentence.
The weakest point is still a decision, not a wording problem. No rewrite fixes the statement until the payment-gap policy is settled. Wording can only describe a promise the product keeps.
What I’d carry forward
Lead with what the competitor can’t copy: a vetted local host and a booking tied to payment.
Replace “no chasing” with a concrete rule, such as booked when the group has paid, released if it hasn’t (once the policy is set).
Drop “one link” from the headline and keep it as a supporting detail.
Name the group size so the statement reflects the narrowed segment.

Working version: Roamly Groups books a vetted local host for your whole party, and the booking is confirmed once everyone has paid their share. You never put the group’s cost on your card.

This is still a hypothesis. I tested it against my own simulation of the competitor, so the real check is showing it to a few organizers and seeing whether they can repeat it back and say what would stop them from using it.

### Final statement
Roamly Groups books a vetted local host for your celebration of 8 to 20 people, and the booking is confirmed once everyone has paid their own share. You plan the trip without ever putting the group’s cost on your card.
