# Messaging Pillars · Roamly Groups

## 1. From/To market shift

- **From, life before:** Planning a group celebration means one person agrees the idea in the group chat, then puts the whole cost on their own card and spends days or weeks chasing friends to pay them back. They track who’s in across a chat, an invite app, and a spreadsheet. Many groups give up and default to a dinner or a bar, because the experience isn’t worth the risk to the planner.
- **To, life after:** The group’s own payments confirm the booking, not one person’s credit card. The organizer stops being the bank and goes back to being a guest at their own celebration, and the group gets a real local experience

## 2. Audiences

- **Number of audiences that need their own message:** 2
- **Why you picked this number:** Audience	Why they need their own message

## 3. Messaging pillars

### Audience 1: Audience 1: The organizer

For: the person planning and paying for a celebration trip (bachelor/bachelorette weekends, milestone birthdays) for 8 to 20 people.

Pillar message:

Roamly Groups books a vetted local host for your celebration, and the booking is confirmed once everyone has paid their own share. You plan the trip without ever putting the group’s cost on your card.
- **Value prop:** Audience 1: The organizer

Promise (lead with this):

Plan the celebration without being the bank.

The change the customer gets:

Before: You commit the group’s money first, then spend days chasing friends and wondering who’s actually coming.
After: The booking is confirmed by the group’s own payments. You stay a guest at your own celebration instead of the person carrying the risk.
- **Capabilities:** #	Capability	What it does	Why the promise needs it
1	Pay-your-share checkout	Each member is charged only their own portion, and the organizer’s card is never charged for anyone else	This is the promise itself: the organizer doesn’t front the group’s money
2	Payment-linked confirmation	The booking is confirmed by the group’s payments reaching a defined threshold, not by one person’s card	The one thing the free stack can’t do, since it has no inventory or host link
3	Deadline and outcome rules	A set payment deadline, with a defined result if someone hasn’t paid (booking releases, shrinks, or a gap policy applies)	Without this, “never on your card” is a claim, not a guarantee. Depends on the unpaid-share policy.
4	Live paid-status view	The organizer sees who’s in, who’s paid, and who’s outstanding in one place	Replaces the spreadsheet and the uncertainty about who’s actually coming
5	Automated reminders	Roamly nudges late payers on a schedule, on the organizer’s behalf	Moves the chasing off the organizer. It reduces the social burden but may not eliminate it.
6	Shareable invite link	One link the organizer drops into the group chat, leading to the experience, the host, and each person’s share	The entry point into the existing workflow. Needed to reach the invitee.
7	Account-free invitee payment (not built yet)	Invitees pay without creating an account first	Protects the loop and the “one tap” promise to invitees
8	Group-size and host guardrails	Group-size caps and pacing rules per experience, set with host operations	Keeps quality on the experiences that earned Roamly’s repeat rate

### Audience 2: A friend or family member invited to an 8-to-20 person celebration, who was handed a payment link by someone they know and never chose Roamly.

They’re a participant, not a shopper. The decision in front of them is “do I trust this enough to pay without friction?”
Many are likely new to Roamly. Some may already have accounts, which is an open data question.
They matter beyond the one booking: the acquisition loop depends on whether they pay smoothly and have a good experience.
Their main worry is being asked to pay a platform they didn’t pick, for something they haven’t seen.
- **Value prop:** Promise (lead with this):

Say yes to your friend in one tap.

The change the customer gets:

Before: You get a payment request for something you haven’t seen, in a tool you may not trust, possibly with an account to create first.
After: You see exactly what you’re joining and what your share costs, you pay in seconds, and you’re in without downloading anything.
- **Capabilities:** Capabilities
#	Capability	What it means for the invitee	Status
1	Invite page with experience and host details	You know what you’re paying for before you pay	Part of the shareable link
2	Clear price for your share	No surprises, no awkward follow-up with the organizer	Part of pay-your-share checkout

4	Refund and cancellation terms shown upfront	You trust paying a platform you didn’t choose	Needs the policy finalized

- **Evidence:** No invitee has used Groups yet, so there are no testimonials to cite. Evidence comes in three layers.

Users already try to coordinate group bookings and drop off, so friction at the payment step is real. This shows the pain, not that our flow fixes it.
Roamly’s repeat rate is three times the industry average, which suggests a good experience creates the next purchase. It isn’t proof that invitees will return.

Share of invitees who already have Roamly accounts, so you count only net-new ones.

## 4. Copy across three surfaces

- **Homepage headline:** . Homepage headline

Plan the celebration. Don’t be the bank.

Subhead: Book a vetted local host for your group. Everyone pays their own share, and your card stays out of it.

Leads with: the promise (“Plan the celebration without being the bank”).

Why: A homepage visitor decides in a few seconds. The promise is the one thing the free stack can’t offer, and it describes what the person gets rather than a feature. The subhead then adds the two proof points a skeptic looks for: a real host and a payment rule.
- **Sales one-liner:** 2. Sales one-liner

“Roamly Groups books a vetted local host for your whole group, and it’s confirmed once everyone has paid their own share, so the group’s cost never lands on your card.”

Leads with: the pillar message itself, built on payment-linked confirmation (capability 2).

Why: In a conversation, the first objection is “Venmo’s free.” This line answers it with the one thing Venmo can’t do: tie payment to a confirmed booking with a real host. It’s meant for support, partnership, and host-ops conversations, since the buying motion itself is self-serve.
- **Social post:** 3. Social post

Everyone’s “definitely in” for the weekend. Then the booking needs one card, and it’s yours.

Roamly Groups: everyone pays their own share, and the trip is confirmed once the group has paid. You just show up to your own celebration.

Leads with: the “before” moment from the value prop (committing the group’s money first), then the “after.”

Why: Social is a scroll, not a decision. A recognizable moment earns the pause, and organizers who’ve been through it will see themselves. The mechanism comes second and stays brief.
