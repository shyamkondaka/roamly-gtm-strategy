# Pricing Recommendation · Roamly Groups

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Enter a new market

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** Transaction-based
- **Who pays, and for what:** Roamly earns on each confirmed group booking, as a share of booking value, paid through the existing host take rate. Invitees pay only their share of the experience, with no per-person fee, and the organizer pays no new fee at launch.

## 3. Packaging

- **Tiers:**
  - Tier 1: Standard
  - Who it’s for: Organizers of 8 to 12 who are comfortable with some risk and a firm deadline.
  - What they get: The invite link, pay-your-share checkout, a live paid-status view, automated reminders, and a booking confirmed once the group has paid.
  - If someone doesn’t pay: The booking shrinks or releases at the deadline. The organizer is never charged.
  - Price (placeholder): No organizer fee. It’s funded by the host take rate. Invitees pay nothing beyond their share.
  - Role in the lineup: The entry tier. It removes any barrier to trying Groups and carries the core promise, so the basics are never paywalled.
  - Tier 2: Protected ⭐ (the tier most organizers should pick)
  - Who it’s for: Organizers of 10 to 20 planning a milestone trip with a fixed date.
  - What they get: Everything in Standard, plus a date guarantee. If someone is short at the deadline, the booking stays confirmed and Roamly collects the gap from that member.
  - If someone doesn’t pay: The booking holds, and Roamly follows up with the late payer.
  - Price (placeholder): Roughly 3 to 5% of booking value, capped (about $45 to $75 on a $1,500 booking). Invitees pay nothing extra.
  - Why most should pick it: It’s the version of the promise that matters most to someone with a fixed date: the trip happens whether or not everyone pays on time. It’s also the first place Roamly asks for money, and it’s tied to something the free stack can’t do. It targets the bet’s core buyer, the 10 to 20 person organizer with a high-value booking, where fronting money hurts most.
  - Tier 3: Assisted
  - Who it’s for: Organizers of 15 to 20+ with a high-value booking and a hard date.
  - What they get: Everything in Protected, plus a named support contact, host-confirmed headcount, and flexible deposit and deadline options.
  - If someone doesn’t pay: Same as Protected, with a person handling the follow-up.
  - Price (placeholder): Roughly 6 to 8% of booking value, to be set with Finance. Invitees pay nothing extra.
  - Role in the lineup: The top tier for the highest-need buyer. It’s support, not a sales motion, triggered by group size or booking value.

## 4. Assumption to test

- **The one assumption I would test before launch:** Organizers planning 10 to 20 person trips will pay a visible fee for the date guarantee, meaning they see “booking holds even if someone is short” as worth paying for and not as something Roamly should include.  Why?  The whole pricing recommendation rests on it. Standard is free to the organizer, so the only place Roamly earns beyond the host take rate is Protected, and Protected only works if organizers value risk removal enough to pay for it. The same fee also has to cover the cost of the guarantee, since Roamly collects late payments from members.

## 5. One-page recommendation

Roamly Groups: Pricing Recommendation (V1)

For: Finance, Sales Leadership, Product Marketing | Status: Draft. All fees are placeholders to test.

Recommendation

Price Roamly Groups per confirmed booking with three tiers, launching with 8-to-20 person bachelor/bachelorette and milestone organizers in one or two cities. The goal is entering a new market, so price serves adoption first and revenue second. Invitees pay only their share, with no per-person fee.

Tier	For	Organizer fee (placeholder)	If someone doesn’t pay
Standard	8-12 people	None, funded by host take rate	Booking shrinks or releases. Organizer never charged.
Protected ⭐	10-20 people, fixed date	~3-5% of booking, capped (~$45-75 on $1,500)	Booking holds. Roamly collects the gap from the late payer.
Assisted	15-20+, hard date	~6-8%, set with Finance	Same as Protected, with a named contact
The assumption everything rests on

Organizers of 10-20 person trips will pay a visible fee for the date guarantee, and won’t see it as something Roamly should include. If they won’t, Protected earns nothing, the guarantee becomes an unfunded cost, and revenue falls back on hosts.

What each stakeholder most needs to hear
Finance
Protected makes Roamly the lender of last resort. On a $1,500 booking with 10 people, one missed share is about $150 that Roamly fronts and must collect. The $45-75 fee has to cover expected losses plus collection cost, so I need your loss-rate estimate before any percentage is final.
Standard is funded by hosts, not by us. Please model whether the take rate holds on 8-to-20 person sessions, which cost hosts more effort.
Decide before the pilot: the loss ceiling per booking, the fee range, and who absorbs a gap that can’t be collected. Organizer cash fronted is our health metric, so these have to be set in advance, not argued afterward.
Sales Leadership
No new quota and no sales team. This is self-serve. Assisted is support, triggered at 15+ people or high booking value, not a sales motion.
Corporate is deliberately deferred. It’s the biggest ticket, and giving it up is the main tradeoff of this bet. If team events booked by one person on a company card show up in the celebration data, flag them to me. That is the trigger to reopen corporate.
Your part is the host side. Group-size caps and payment timing for confirmed headcounts need host-network buy-in in the launch cities. Tell me where hosts resist.
Product Marketing
Don’t sell splitting. Venmo, Splitwise, and Partiful give it away, and it invites the “it’s free” comparison. Lead with the promise: plan the celebration without being the bank.
Frame Protected as the date guarantee: the trip happens whether or not everyone pays on time. Frame Standard honestly, since it can shrink or release.
Three claims stay off all copy until they’re true: “never on your card” (needs the unpaid-share policy), “no account needed” (needs the account-free invitee payment, not built yet), and “vetted” (needs a one-line definition of vetting).
Decide with me: whether the guarantee reads as a premium or as the baseline. The test below answers it.
Risks to watch
Organizers treat the guarantee as the default, which forces it into the base price.
Hosts push back on take-rate funding or on payment timing.
Unpaid-share losses exceed what the Protected fee covers.
Assisted is too small for a full tier and works better as an add-on.
How the assumption gets tested

After launch, run three cells: A (Standard only), B (Protected offered at checkout), C (Protected as default, Standard as opt-down). Agree the floors for take-up, completion, and cash exposure with Data and Finance before launch.

Best research method: Conjoint

Choice-based conjoint is the best test of this assumption.

It tests the actual trade-off: guarantee type, fee, support, and group size, shown as realistic tier choices. That isolates how much the guarantee itself is worth.
Including a “Standard, no fee” option and a “book elsewhere” option shows whether people trade up or expect the guarantee included.
It simulates take-up and revenue at different fee levels, which is what Finance needs.

Why not the others: Van Westendorp gives an acceptable price range for one product but can’t isolate the guarantee. Max-Diff ranks feature importance but has no price, so it can’t show willingness to pay.

Practical notes: it needs a survey sample of organizers in the hundreds, so recruit from the workaround cohort and recent group bookers. Show the fee next to the amount they would otherwise front. Treat stated intent as directional and confirm it with the live pilot cells.
